From blank cells to a score

The eleven steps that turn 17 spreadsheets of UN data into one number per country–year, what each step's method is called, and why that method and not a simpler one.

197 countries · 2015–2023 · 524 series · 1,773 country–years

Steps 1–3 below decide what is measured, steps 4–7 are the estimator, steps 8–9 try to break it, and steps 10–11 publish it with its own weaknesses attached.

STEP01

The outcome is a single bit

Other SDG measures read the values countries report. This one reads whether a value is there at all:

y( country, year, series) = { 1if a value was reported 0otherwise

Nothing downstream looks at the magnitude of a reported figure. A country that publishes unflattering numbers scores high; a country that publishes nothing scores low. The unit of analysis is a country–year, and each carries 524 ones and zeros.

STEP02

Complete the grid, so the zeros exist

The UN export holds a row only where a country reported something. A missing report is an absent row, not a zero. Load the export and count, and you count only successes. So the first operation builds every cell that could exist — 197 countries × 9 years × every series — and marks what the export does not reach as 0.

Completed grid
The full country × year × series rectangle with zeros filled in. 1,179,045 cells, of which 38.2% are reported. Built once by irt_prep.R, which every model sources, so no two fits can disagree about what the data are.
The failure this prevents

Skip it and “reporting” silently becomes “reporting, among countries that reported” — the sample defined by the outcome. Everything downstream would then be wrong in the same invisible direction.

STEP03

Choose the series: 665 → 607 → 524

The 2–98% filter 665 → 607
Series reported in fewer than 2% or more than 98% of country–years are removed. If almost every country reports a series, learning that one particular country reported it tells you nothing about that country — everyone did. The same is true in reverse when almost nobody reports it. Either way the series cannot tell countries apart, so it is no use for placing them.
Counting repeats once 607 → 524
Some measurements are entered in the database more than once — children stunted appears as a percentage and, separately, as a count in thousands. Counting both pays a country twice for one reporting decision. 59 such groups of repeats contribute one series each.

Two conditions must both hold before a group is counted as one. The series must be reported, and not reported, together in every one of the 1,773 country–years; and their descriptions must show they measure the same thing a different way. The pattern alone is not enough, because at these reporting rates unrelated series coincide by chance: forest certification, electricity access and refugee counts all move together in 99.5% of country–years without measuring anything in common.

Where the line is drawn

Series that merely resemble one another are kept apart, because a country publishing one and withholding the other has made a real reporting choice — exactly the thing being measured. The cost of that decision is disclosed in step 10 as weight concentration. The test re–runs on every fit and halts it if a future data release breaks it.

STEP04

The estimator: a two–parameter logistic item response model

This is what makes the measure more than a tally. The design comes from testing: a student's ability is unobserved, but you see which questions they answered, and hard questions are more informative than easy ones. Swap student for country–year and question for series. Hollyer, Rosendorff and Vreeland introduced this design for World Development Indicators; it is applied here to SDG data.

P(y=1 ∣θ) = 1 1+ e −( ajθ+ dj)
θ — the latent trait
“Latent” means not directly observable. θ is a country–year's propensity to report: never seen, inferred from 524 ones and zeros. This is the score.
aj — discrimination
How sharply series j separates high–propensity country–years from low ones. This is the weight the series carries, and the model estimates it from the data. Nobody assigns it.
dj — intercept, or difficulty
How much propensity it takes before a country–year reports that series at all.
Why this and not “percent of series reported”

A percentage gives every series equal weight and has no error bar. It also needs a denominator chosen by hand: all series in the framework, or only those the UN opened a cell for. Seven such measures built for this project disagreed badly, two correlating at only 0.36. Estimating each series' difficulty is what removes that judgement call — and the index correlates only 0.61–0.68 with share–based measures, so the two are not interchangeable.

The assumption to know about

Local independence: conditional on θ, the 524 series are independent of one another. This is not exactly true, since related series are reported together. Step 9 is built to attack it rather than assert it.

STEP05

Fit the series parameters by EM

Every a and d must be estimated before any country can be scored, and θ is unobserved — so you cannot estimate the series from the countries or the countries from the series. The standard answer is to alternate.

Marginal maximum likelihood, via the EM algorithm
Guess the series parameters; work out the implied distribution of θ for every country–year (expectation); re–estimate the series parameters as if that distribution were data (maximisation); repeat. Each pass is a cycle. “Marginal” means θ is integrated out rather than estimated alongside, so the parameter count does not grow with countries.
Convergence tolerance
Stop when no parameter moves by more than this in a cycle. Set to 1e−3.

Three settings depart from the defaults, and each was forced by a failed run. All are recorded, with the run that established them, in METHODS_LOG.md — including the attempts that did not work.

SettingValueWhy not the default
acceleratesquarem Roughly 20× fewer cycles. On the default, the 17–factor model ran past 19 hours without finishing. Binary fits only: it breaks ordered thresholds.
TOL1e−3 The usual 1e−4 is unreachable here: 13 of 1,569 parameters drift along a ridge where the likelihood is flat. Checked rather than assumed — against the strict run, no score moves by more than 0.0065, against standard errors of 0.3–0.5.
prior on anorm(0, 2) Needed only for the 17–factor model. A few perfectly collinear series make a goal's factor an exact copy of them, so their slope has no finite value — discriminations ran past 80 and never settled. A normal prior bounds them. Not lognormal: 172 of 1,045 loadings are negative, which a lognormal forbids.

The overall index converges in 187 cycles, about 25 seconds, and reproduces identically on an independent run.

STEP06

Score each country–year, with an error bar

With the series parameters fixed, a country–year's 524 bits imply a probability distribution over its θ. The score is the mean of that distribution.

θ^= ∫θ L(y∣θ) φ(θ) dθ ∫ L(y∣θ) φ(θ) dθ
EAP — expected a posteriori
The posterior mean of θ. L is the likelihood of the observed pattern of ones and zeros; φ is a standard normal prior, which is also what fixes the scale at mean 0, SD 1. The integral is computed over a grid of θ values.
The standard error
The standard deviation of that same posterior. It falls out of the same calculation, which is why every score here carries an error bar and none were added afterwards. These are genuine posterior standard deviations, not asymptotic approximations. Median on the overall index: 0.114.
Why EAP and not maximum likelihood

For a country–year that reported everything, or nothing, the maximum likelihood estimate is ±infinity. EAP is always finite because the prior pulls it back, and it returns a standard error for free. The cost is slight shrinkage toward 0 at the extremes.

Orientation. The model identifies the factor only up to sign: flip every a and every θ and the likelihood is identical. So after fitting, the sign is set by correlating θ with the plain count of series reported, and flipped if needed. More reporting is always a higher score.

STEP07

Three scores from the same estimator

Steps 4–6 are run more than once. Nothing about the method changes; only how many factors the model has and which series load on them.

ColumnModelReads as
vsiOne factor, all 524 series, one fit How much this country–year reports overall
goal_levelSixteen separate one–factor fits, each on one goal's own series How much it reports on that goal, overall level included
goal_relativeOne bifactor fit: 17 factors, general plus one per goal — not in this release That goal net of the country's own overall level

Each carries a matching _se. Because a goal fit uses 17–75 series rather than 524, goal–level error bars are about three times the overall index's, which is why every goal is published with its own. Goal 7 is listed with no score rather than omitted. Its framework has only six series, the fewest of any goal; three of them are reported by more than 98% of country–years and are removed by the step–3 filter, leaving too few to identify a factor.

Bifactor model
Every series loads on exactly two factors: the general one, shared by all 524, and the specific one for its own goal. The general factor absorbs the country's overall reporting; what remains on the specific factor is the part of that goal's reporting the overall level does not explain.
Why a bifactor and not a subtraction

goal_level minus vsi looks like the same quantity and is not. Subtracting two correlated estimates gives a difference whose error is larger than either one's and whose scale means nothing in particular. The bifactor estimates the residual directly, as a parameter, so it arrives with its own standard error.

Divergence is the hypothesis, not a fault

Across the sixteen goals, a median 64% of the variation in goal–level reporting is not explained by the country's overall level — from 18% for goal 17 to almost all of it for goals 6 and 15. Reporting is not one–dimensional, so the goal layer is never checked against, or gated on, agreement with the overall index.

STEP08

First check: how precise is a score?

Two different uncertainties, easily confused. The posterior standard error from step 6 covers only the first.

Reliability
The share of the spread in scores that is signal rather than measurement noise: var(score) var(score)+ mean(SE2) Overall index 0.987. Goal level: median 0.844, range 0.772–0.953. 0.70 (Nunnally & Bernstein 1994) is quoted as a reference point, never applied as a cut.
The bootstrap
Resample the 1,773 country–years with replacement, refit the whole model, re–score the original rows, repeat. The spread across replicates is how much the scores would move had a different sample been drawn — uncertainty in the series parameters, which the posterior standard error does not include.

On the goal layer, 500 replicates: none failed, and the bootstrap standard deviation is 7–42% of the posterior standard error, median 13%. A ratio below 1 means the published standard error already covers parameter uncertainty with room to spare, so the posterior standard error is the conservative one to report — and it is the one published.

STEP09

Second check: is it measuring something real?

Precision is not validity. A score can be measured very precisely and still be nothing but a restatement of its own inputs. Three checks, each able to fail.

Held–out series

Held–out series validity
Fit on 70% of a goal's series, then ask how well the resulting score predicts reporting on the 30% it never saw. Scored by the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC): the probability that a randomly chosen reported case outranks a randomly chosen unreported one. AUC= 1n1n0 ∑i∈reported ∑j∈not reported 1[ θ^i > θ^j ] Pair every reported case with every unreported one and count how often the reported one scored higher; n1 and n0 are how many of each there are. 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect. Computed per held–out series, then the median, against a permutation null drawn fresh for each series by shuffling the scores.

Median AUC 0.725 against a null sitting on 0.500. Thirteen of sixteen goals reach 0.649 or better. The three weakest — goal 17 (0.618), goal 15 (0.526) and goal 6 (0.479) — are exactly the ones already set apart on content grounds in step 10, which the test knew nothing about.

Why this is the check that matters

It is the only one that can falsify the local independence assumption from step 4. If the score were merely a summary of the series fed into it, it would have no purchase on series it was never shown, and the AUC would fall to the null. Prediction is not the goal here; prediction is being used as a test.

What these checks do not settle

SPI and log GDP per capita together explain 82% of the index, and only 4.8% of its total variance is within–country. This release therefore cannot separate reporting withheld by choice from reporting absent through incapacity. That separation needs the provenance layer on the roadmap. What this release claims is coverage of the SDG era and resolution by domain, and no more.

STEP10

Disclose, do not gate

Every goal that fits is published. Nothing is dropped for imprecision. Each goal instead carries four numbers a reader can weigh it by, so the judgement stays with the reader.

ColumnWhat it warns about
reliabilityHow much of the spread is signal (0.772–0.953)
se_medianThe typical error bar on that goal's scores
weight_top5_pctShare of the score carried by its five heaviest series — the cost of the step–3 decision. Goal 10 is 80%, goal 17 only 23%
weight_agency_pctShare resting on series the custodian agency produces rather than the country. Goal 3 is 94%, goal 2 91%, ten goals under 7%
Two goals are flagged on content, for different reasons

Goal 6 is largely satellite measurement of lakes, wetlands and river flow, so “reported” there mostly records whether an agency published a dataset rather than a country's choice.

Goal 15 is overwhelmingly country–produced, but a third of its series are treaty and policy instruments (e.g. Nagoya Protocol, Aichi targets, NBSAP), which may track commitment status rather than reporting effort.

Neither is a precision problem, and no error bar can warn a reader about either, so the flag travels in the data. Both goals are published in full.

STEP11

Pin the version, then nothing moves

The run order is fixed and documented in code/README.md: build the variant map; fit the overall index and the sixteen goal fits, which are independent of each other; characterise who produced each series; build this site's data; run the external validity checks; print every number the paper quotes. Both fits source irt_prep.R, which halts if the variant map is missing or stale, so the two layers cannot drift apart.

A release copies the scores, the per–series parameters, the diagnostics, the run log and the code into a versioned folder and deposits it at Zenodo, where each version keeps its own DOI and the concept DOI always resolves to the latest.

Terms, one line each

Series
One SDG data series — the thing a country either reports or does not. What the model treats as an item.
Latent trait (θ)
The unobserved propensity to report, inferred from 524 ones and zeros. The score itself.
Factor
One latent dimension in a model. The overall index has one; the bifactor has seventeen.
2PL
Two–parameter logistic: each series gets a discrimination and an intercept.
Discrimination (a)
How sharply a series separates high from low propensity. The weight it earns.
Intercept (d)
How much propensity it takes to report that series at all. Its difficulty.
Local independence
The assumption that, given θ, series are independent. Tested in step 9, not asserted.
EM algorithm
Alternating expectation and maximisation passes that estimate series parameters while θ is unobserved. One pass is a cycle.
EAP
Expected a posteriori: the posterior mean of θ. The scoring rule; its posterior SD is the standard error.
Bifactor
A model where each series loads on exactly two factors: one general, one for its own goal.
Reliability
Signal share of score variance: var(score) / (var(score) + mean SE²).
Bootstrap
Resample country–years, refit, re–score, repeat. Measures uncertainty in the series parameters.
AUC
Area under the receiver operating characteristic curve: the chance that a reported case outranks an unreported one. 0.5 is a coin flip.
Permutation null
The AUC from shuffled scores — the floor any real result must clear.

Every setting named here is recorded in METHODS_LOG.md with the run that established it, including the runs that failed. Fitted with mirt 1.48 on R 4.5.3.
