The World on Record

The first transparency measure covering the SDG era: latent reporting behaviour across 607 statistical series and 197 countries, 2015–2023, with uncertainty quantified for every country–year.

The World on Record Index (TWR) is the overall score published here. Sector–level scores follow when the goal model finishes estimating.

Existing transparency indices stop in 2015. This one begins there, and resolves reporting by policy domain rather than returning a single national score. Estimated by item response theory on the UN Global SDG Database.

What this measures

Reporting is a choice, not just a capacity

For every country, series and year, the UN database either holds a published figure or it does not. Treat each of the 607 statistical series as an item on a test and each country–year as a test–taker: a country's underlying willingness and ability to report is then estimated jointly with how demanding each series is. This is the design Hollyer, Rosendorff and Vreeland introduced for World Development Indicators, applied here to SDG data.

The point of the model is that it removes a judgement call. Every simpler measure of missing data has to divide by a denominator chosen by hand — all series in the framework, or only those the UN opened a cell for. The first assumes every country should report what the richest ones do; the second reacts to reporting itself. Seven such measures built for this project disagreed badly, two of them correlating at only 0.36. Estimating each series' difficulty instead of assuming a denominator is what resolves that.

Scores are on a standard–normal scale: 0 is the average country–year, positive is more complete reporting, and each estimate carries its own uncertainty. They are posterior means, so the error bars are genuine posterior standard deviations rather than asymptotic approximations.

What this release does and does not do

The attribution layer is the point of the project, and it is not in the scores published here. This page carries the composite index only. Telling withheld data from absent capacity requires the provenance split, which is in progress.

The consequence is visible in the rankings: small island states with genuine capacity constraints currently sit beside governments that are plainly choosing silence. Separating those two is the work, not an oversight in it.

Mean score by year

Read 2023 with care

The series rises steadily through 2020 and then falls sharply in 2023. That last drop is very likely an artefact rather than a finding: the UN database fills recent years in gradually, and this snapshot was taken before 2023 had settled. Small states with genuine capacity constraints appear alongside deliberate non–reporters at the bottom of that year for the same reason.

Any analysis using these scores should either drop 2023 or absorb the lag through year effects. Treating it as a normal year would read an archival delay as a collapse in global transparency.

By policy domain

Which areas went quiet

A single national score says a government reports less. It cannot say what it stopped reporting. Scores resolved to the 16 SDG goals with enough series to support them can — and roughly half the variation in goal–level reporting is not explained by a country's overall level, so the domains move substantially on their own.

Method and status

How it is built, and what is missing

Estimation
Two–parameter logistic item response model, fitted by marginal maximum likelihood with an EM algorithm; scores are expected a posteriori estimates. The reported fit converged rather than exhausting its iteration cap, and reproduced identically on an independent run.
Item set
607 of 665 series survive a filter removing those reported in fewer than 2% or more than 98% of country–years, which carry no information for separating countries.
Deliberately excluded
No democracy score, income group, population or region enters the model. Those are explanatory variables in the accompanying study, and including them would make any subsequent test circular.
Goal–level scores
Estimated by a bifactor model — one general factor plus one per SDG goal with enough series to identify it — and not yet published on this page. See the roadmap below.
Not yet smoothed
Scores are estimated independently by year. A dynamic prior linking adjacent years, as in the original transparency index, is deferred: nine years is a short series for a random walk to earn its place.

Roadmap

What each version measures

Three dimensions of reporting behaviour are separable in the SDG database and are being added in sequence. Each is listed with what it covers, because a measure's scope is a fact users need rather than a caveat to be buried.

v1

Domain — this release

Whether a government published anything for each of 607 series, resolved into an overall score and a score per SDG goal. Answers which policy areas fell silent, not merely that a country did. No existing transparency index disaggregates this way.

v2

Provenance

The database records who produced each figure — a national statistical office or an international agency — and why a cell is empty: suppressed, structurally inapplicable, or unexplained. Separating those is what distinguishes a government withholding data from one that cannot produce it. The World Development Indicators record neither, which is why no earlier measure can draw the line.

v3

Depth

A national total and a full breakdown by sex, age and location currently score alike. 36% of series admit disaggregation, and depth correlates with the index at 0.57 once the series set is held constant — so it carries signal the binary discards. It enters as ordered responses the model estimates over, not as a ratio computed after the fact.

Taken together the three produce a measure that can tell silence by choice from silence by incapacity, and locate it by domain. That is the destination; this page documents how far along the way it currently is.
